# B014.6 — Job worker and compute lifetime conformance

Date: 2026-10-20

## Contract exercised

The pinned `databricks-sdk==0.153.0` local loopback flow executes actual fresh Python workers under each of the seven selected job shapes in document 16: serverless mapped environment, classic standard new/existing/shared, and classic dedicated new/existing/shared. Each worker reports its resolved profile, attempt ID, selected resource identity/generation where applicable, process ID, and module-local state. The tests verify successful attempt state; new/shared resources terminate after all consumers release; shared tasks use one identity and two consumer lifetimes; and existing all-purpose resources keep the same ID and autostart when requested.

Separate POSIX integration cases restart and terminate an existing all-purpose cluster while a worker and child process are running. The job becomes cancelled from the observed worker termination, both processes are reaped, and the pre-existing Delta table remains readable at version 0 before and after workspace reopen. The service-crash recovery case kills the service and worker, reopens the workspace, reports the unobserved attempt interrupted with no exit code, retains earlier output, and releases the run-scoped compute consumer without inferring job success from its resource record.

## Limits

All infrastructure identities and lifecycle states are simulated. The workers execute plain local Python, with no Spark/Sparkless session and no worker-facing table-service API. The Delta preservation case reads an already committed table across compute interruption; it does not claim that a job worker created or mutated that table. The test corpus does not establish Databricks runtime/access-mode parity, real serverless infrastructure, external process side effects exactly once, or Windows descendant cleanup.
